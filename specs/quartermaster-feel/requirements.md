# Requirements — The Quartermaster's Feel (TD-102)

> **R370+**, **P168+**, **T381+**. Author brief, 2026-08-09.
> **A polish pass on TD-101, not a redesign.** The data model, the interaction flow and the
> architecture are preserved; this changes how the room *reads*.

---

## What a fresh session needs to know

TD-101 made the Quartermaster a room. It works: instruments stand on shelves, selecting carries one
to a counter, packing flies it into the case, and the rite seals it. **None of that is re-opened.**
`gear.ts` → `Catalog`, `lore.gd`, the state machine, the carry, `fly_in`, `seal_rite.gd` and the
4-slot capacity all stay exactly as they are.

**The finding that shaped this pass.** The brief's §5 asks to "remove the large conventional UI arrow
as the primary hover indicator." There is no such arrow in the code — hover is a lift plus a
brighten. Measuring the icon sheet found what the brief was actually looking at: **`cantors-ear` is
204 opaque pixels in 4 colours, dominated by gold `(166,128,58)`** — the loudest and most golden
object on the shelf, and at 24px it reads as an arrow. So the "arrow" is an *icon*, and it is
simultaneously a **§16 gold-discipline violation**: gold is supposed to mean authority, selection and
the seal, not an ordinary item sitting on a shelf.

**The toolchain split this pass turns on** (TD-057, settled by measurement): **Aseprite owns
sprites** — anything where a pixel is a design decision, which is exactly the icons, the counter
props and the shelf stock. **Python owns surfaces** — wood, stone, parchment, bevels, 9-slices,
anything feeding a shader. The brief's §17 ("authored PNGs, not procedural generation in engine")
agrees with this and does not conflict with it: the generators emit PNGs offline; **no pixel art is
generated by Godot at run time**, then or now.

## R370 — The background supports the room, it does not compete

- AC: lower contrast, larger and subtler masonry, darker recesses, slight irregularity.
- AC: the shelves, counter, parchment and pack remain visually dominant; the wall must never be the
  first thing the eye lands on.

## R371 — The counter is the visual heart, and looks worked at

- AC: three zones — **left** ledger/quill/ink/paperwork, **centre** the inspected instrument,
  **right** candle, seal/stamp, balance, sealing wax.
- AC: props are **decorative**: not focusable, not clickable, not tooltipped. Only the instrument and
  the actions respond.
- AC: the empty middle of the counter reads as a working surface, not as unused space.

## R372 — Shelves read as an organised store, not slots in disguise

- AC: subtle variation — boxes, books, folded cloth, containers, supports, **different heights**, and
  **occasional deliberate gaps**.
- AC: **organised, never scattered**: each category stays in its own unit and instruments stay evenly
  pitched. Variation is in the dressing, not in the instruments' placement.
- AC: instruments sit on visible **stands/bases** where it helps them read as placed rather than
  floating.
- AC: category plaques read as **physical wooden/brass/parchment labels fixed to the shelf**.

## R373 — Hover says "this has caught my attention"

- AC: a restrained **Collegium-gold outline** plus slight warm illumination, readable at low
  resolution.
- AC: no modern glow, no neon, no particle burst, no large arrow or marker.
- AC: **the icons are re-authored in Aseprite** so no ordinary instrument is the goldest thing on
  screen (R376), which is what made hover unreadable in the first place.

## R374 — The record is a physical Collegium document

- AC: hierarchy — **name** large and prominent; **classification** smaller, gold; the question;
  body text; and a **WARNING** block in muted burgundy under its own heading.
- AC: it keeps every word `lore.gd` already authors. No re-writing of item prose.
- AC: restrained texture only — no over-texturing, no drop-shadow-as-decoration.

## R375 — The pack, its status, and the departure read as issued equipment

- AC: status wording is immersive: `EXPEDITION PACK — 1 / 4`, not debug-like inventory terms.
- AC: **SEAL & DEPART** is subdued and low-contrast while the pack cannot be issued, and takes
  restrained Collegium gold with a stronger border when it can. Never flashy.
- AC: capacity stays **4**. The seal ritual stays exactly as TD-101 shipped it.

## R376 — Gold means something

- AC: strongest gold is reserved for active selection, important headings, Collegium insignia, the
  seal, and the ready/departure state.
- AC: ordinary furniture and inactive controls use dark brown, charcoal, muted bronze, desaturated
  brass and parchment.

## R377 — Ambience: occupied and old, not supernatural

- AC: warm candlelight variation and **a few floating dust motes**. No fog.
- AC: within the TD-101 budget — **≤ 20 particles**, no full-frame additive layer, no `_process`.

## R378 (containment) — nothing that works is re-opened

- AC: **no `src/**` change.** No change to item data, capacity, valid/invalid states, seal state,
  depart, or keyboard/mouse support.
- AC: **no pixel art generated by Godot at run time**; all art is authored offline as PNGs and
  imported **Nearest**, filtering never introduced.
- AC: no text overlap, no clipped parchment or shelves, no cramped slots, no floating elements.
- AC: the Contract Board stays provably unchanged (worktree diff, ~0.47% control floor).

## Correctness Properties

- **P168 (gold is scarce):** no ordinary shelf instrument is among the most-gold objects on screen;
  the seal, the ready action and active selection are.
- **P169 (props are scenery):** every counter prop and every piece of shelf stock is
  `MOUSE_FILTER_IGNORE` and unfocusable — the room never offers an affordance it will not honour.
- **P170 (nearest everywhere):** every station PNG imports with filtering off.
- **P171 (the flow is untouched):** select → carry → record → pack → seal → depart behaves exactly as
  TD-101 shipped it, and the budget checks still pass.
